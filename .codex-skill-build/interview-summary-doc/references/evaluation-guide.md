## Evidence Hierarchy

Rank evidence in this order:

1. Direct observed behavior in the interview
2. Specific examples the candidate explained with detail and tradeoffs
3. Candidate claims without supporting detail
4. Interviewer intuition without explicit evidence

Prefer higher-ranked evidence when summarizing. If the document relies mainly on lower-ranked evidence, say so.

## Recommended Dimensions

Use these default dimensions when the company rubric is missing:

- Technical depth: correctness, depth of reasoning, tradeoff awareness, debugging quality
- Problem solving: decomposition, prioritization, iteration, handling ambiguity
- Communication: clarity, structure, listening, responsiveness to feedback
- Collaboration: stakeholder awareness, teamwork examples, conflict handling
- Ownership: initiative, follow-through, accountability, scope management
- Role fit: match to level, domain relevance, motivation, likely ramp speed

Do not force every summary to use every dimension. Drop dimensions that have no signal.

## Recommendation Calibration

- `strong hire`: repeated high-quality evidence across the critical dimensions, low unresolved risk
- `hire`: enough signal to support hiring, with manageable risks
- `mixed`: meaningful positives and meaningful concerns, or inconsistent evidence across interviewers
- `no hire`: evidence shows significant gaps in one or more critical dimensions
- `insufficient signal`: source material is too thin or too inconsistent for a fair decision

Do not convert a weakly supported `hire` into `strong hire` just because the notes are enthusiastic.

## Writing Patterns

Prefer evidence-first sentences like these:

- `Showed strong problem decomposition by breaking the API redesign into data model, migration, and rollout phases.`
- `Communication signal was mixed: explanations were clear after prompting, but initial answers were often too high-level.`
- `Ownership remains uncertain because the examples emphasized team delivery without clarifying personal scope.`

Avoid weak phrasing like these:

- `Seems smart and experienced.`
- `Probably a good fit.`
- `Good communication.`

When the evidence conflicts, use a pattern like:

`Signal on technical depth was inconsistent. One round showed solid tradeoff reasoning, while another exposed gaps in debugging speed under time pressure.`

## Red Lines

Never include or infer evaluation from protected or non-job-related attributes, including age, gender, race, nationality, family status, health, religion, or appearance.

Do not:

- invent scores, examples, or quotes
- present interviewer emotion as evidence
- write external-facing feedback in the same tone as internal hiring notes
- hide disagreement between interviewers when it affects the decision
